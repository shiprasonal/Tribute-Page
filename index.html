1931 – 2015

Dr. A. P. J. Abdul Kalam

The boy from Rameswaram who taught a nation to dream bigger.

Read his story

A Life of Quiet Brilliance

Avul Pakir Jainulabdeen Abdul Kalam was born on 15 October 1931 in Rameswaram, a small island town in Tamil Nadu. His family had little money, and as a boy he sold newspapers to help at home. Even then, teachers remembered him as a curious student who asked questions and never stopped wanting to learn.

That curiosity took him to Madras Institute of Technology, where he studied aerospace engineering. He later joined India's defence research and space organisations. He worked on rockets and satellite launch vehicles, and became one of the people who helped India stand on its own feet in space and missile technology.

Kalam never treated science as something only for laboratories. He believed it should serve ordinary people. That is why he worked on a low-cost heart stent with a cardiologist, and why he spent so much time talking with school children, asking them what they wanted to build in the world.

In 2002 he became the 11th President of India, and people began calling him the "People's President". He stayed simple and approachable in a very grand office. He kept writing, teaching and travelling until his last day, when he collapsed while giving a lecture to students in Shillong in July 2015. It felt like the way he would have chosen to go: among young minds.

Milestones

  1. 1931

    Born in Rameswaram

    Grows up in a modest family on the island coast of Tamil Nadu.

  2. 1960

    Graduates in aerospace engineering

    Completes his studies at Madras Institute of Technology and begins his career in defence research.

  3. 1980

    Rohini satellite reaches orbit

    As project director of the SLV-III programme, he helps India launch its own satellite using an indigenous rocket.

  4. 1997

    Bharat Ratna

    Receives India's highest civilian honour for his work in science and technology.

  5. 1998

    Pokhran-II and the Kalam-Raju stent

    Serves as chief project coordinator, along with R. Chidambaram, for the Pokhran-II nuclear tests, and co-develops the low-cost Kalam-Raju coronary stent with a cardiologist.

  6. 2002

    Becomes President of India

    Serves as the 11th President until 2007 and wins the affection of millions, especially students.

  7. 2015

    A final lecture

    Passes away on 27 July 2015 at IIM Shillong, while doing what he loved most: teaching.
